# Alpine style

## Palette
- Walls: stone_bricks and cobblestone ground floor; spruce_planks / stripped_spruce_log, white_terracotta plaster above
- Frame: spruce_log, dark_oak_log, stripped_spruce_log for balconies
- Roof: spruce_stairs / dark_oak_stairs, with snow_block or snow layers on top; stone_brick_slab for tiles
- Floor: spruce_planks, stone_bricks, cobblestone; gravel and packed dirt_path outside
- Trim: spruce_fence balcony rails, spruce_trapdoor shutters, white_concrete window frames
- Accents: red / white carpet and banners, barrels of firewood (oak_log stack), hay_block
- Lighting: lantern on posts and hanging under balcony beams, campfire, wall torches inside
- Plants: spruce trees, ferns, short_grass, white and blue flowers, snow layers on ground

## Proportions
- Storey height 4; ground floor of stone 4 high, wooden storeys above.
- Roof pitch shallow to medium (1 up per 2 across) so snow stays; overhang 2-3 blocks, deep eaves.
- Balcony 1-2 blocks deep wrapped around the upper storey.
- Window rhythm: paired 2x2 windows with shutters every 4 blocks, flower boxes under them.
- Chalet is wider than tall; ridge runs across the slope.

## Typical details
- Large stone chimney, firewood stacks against the wall, hay loft door.
- Stepped stone terraces and stairs up the slope, retaining walls of cobblestone.
- Snow only on top surfaces (roofs, ground, ledges); never snow on the underside or floating.
- Bell tower or small chapel with a steep spruce spire.

## Common mistakes
1. Snow layers floating or on sides; they need a solid block below.
2. Steep roofs with no overhang; alpine roofs are wide and shallow.
3. Houses built flat on a slope; set each on terraces with stone bases that follow the ground.
4. Birch and oak trees among the snow; use spruce.
5. Balconies with no support posts or floor; fences must stand on something.
