# Japanese style

## Palette
- Frame / posts / beams: dark_oak_log, stripped_dark_oak_log, spruce_log, dark_oak_planks
- Walls: calcite or white_concrete plaster between the frame; spruce_trapdoor / paper-screen look with white_stained_glass_pane
- Roof: deepslate_tile_stairs / slabs / deepslate_tiles; ridge in deepslate_tile_slab; gable trim dark_oak
- Base: stone_bricks, cobblestone, andesite, mossy_stone_bricks (ishigaki stone base, slightly battered)
- Floor: spruce_planks, stripped_spruce_log; inside tatami = green_carpet over spruce_planks or moss_carpet
- Trim: dark_oak_fence, dark_oak_trapdoor (open, as shutters), stripped_dark_oak_wood
- Accents: red_concrete / red_terracotta (vermilion torii, bridges), gold_block sparingly, bell
- Lighting: lantern (hanging from beams), stone toro = stone_brick_wall + lantern + stone_brick_slab cap, campfire for hearth
- Plants: autumn maples = azalea_leaves and flowering_azalea_leaves for red-orange crowns on dark oak trunks, plus cherry_leaves, bamboo, moss_carpet, fern; gravel on the ground

## Proportions
- Storey height 4 blocks inside (floor to ceiling 3 free blocks plus beam row).
- Roof pitch low and wide, 1 block up per 2 across for hip/gable; eaves curve up one block at the corners.
- Overhang 2-3 blocks; deep eaves are the style. Supports (fence posts) under long eaves.
- Window rhythm: a 2-wide shoji panel every 3 blocks between frame posts; posts every 3-4 blocks.
- Base plinth 1-2 blocks of stone under the frame; steps up to a raised veranda (engawa).

## Typical details
- Torii: two dark red posts 4 high, kasagi cap wider than posts by 1, nuki bar below; stand it on a gravel sando path.
- Stone toro lanterns along paths and at gates; gravel sando paths with stepping stones.
- Ishigaki stone base walls with a gentle slope; white plaster walls above.
- Curved eaves: stairs up at the corners, slabs on the flat; pagoda roofs shrink each storey.
- Smooth stair roads with flat landings, earth banks and moss at the sides.

## Common mistakes
1. Steep European pitched roofs; Japanese roofs are low, wide and curved up at the corners.
2. Bright oak planks and cobblestone; keep to dark wood, white plaster and grey stone.
3. No overhang, so the roof looks like a lid; make the eaves 2-3 blocks.
4. Torii or toro floating on a path; set them on the ground or a stone base.
5. Glass windows instead of paper-screen panels and open shutters.

## Reference: approved Japanese town
A hillside town of dark oak and spruce framed houses with calcite plaster walls, steep-sided but low-pitched deepslate tile roofs whose eaves curve up at the corners, set on stone ishigaki bases. A vermilion torii stands at the foot of a gravel sando path that climbs with smooth stair roads: each rise is a bottom stair facing uphill, with flat landings at every turn and gentle curves. Stone toro lanterns line the path, autumn maples in red and orange lean over earth banks (not tall walls) beside the roads, and every room and doorway is lit by hanging lanterns. Doors are clear, plants sit on grass, nothing floats.
